# Review: Larkspur Tool Library discussion guide

Verdict: CHANGES NEEDED

Most of the first review is resolved: Q4, Q8, the Q7 probe, the timing and the card wording are all fixed, and the new Q6 is a big improvement. The guide is still easy to run: it asks for stories, has no jargon, puts facts before the one opinion question, and the timing is now believable (34 minutes, inside the 40 allowed).
Two of the follow-up questions under Q6 (the new ones added to fix Goal 2) lean the same way the old Q4 and Q6 did, so they need a small rewrite. The consent script also cannot be read as it stands until the committee answers; that is a committee task, not a wording fault.
`guide-check` and `lint` found nothing (33 questions and probes read, 0 flags); the findings below come from reading every question myself.

It is the same assumption that was removed from Q4 and Q6, and a follow-up should stay neutral. | M3, M1, M2 | "Tell me about any time borrowing from the shelf crossed your mind since then." ## Other findings (not about one question's wording)
These did not fit the flag column, which allows only leading, double-barrelled, jargon, yes/no and long.
- **Consent script, still waiting for the committee.** The listener line and the delete-the-recording line are correctly held back in brackets. Until the committee answers the "[ask them: ...]" marker, Mara has to read a shorter script and say only what has been agreed. The lead should get that answer before the first interview. The script otherwise covers recording, stopping, privacy and "no right or wrong answers" (M2: "ask permission to record the interview and take notes"; "there are no wrong answers"). Note also that the unbracketed line "Your name will not be used in anything we write or share" is a promise too; it looks safe and it matches the closing, but the committee should see it.
- **Q7 and its goals.** The guide says Q7 ("Walk me through a typical week for you.") serves Goals 2 and 3. A typical week is a good, neutral opener, but on its own it will not say why someone stopped or never joined; the answer will have to come from the follow-ups and Q8. Not a fault, just a thin link. Keep, but do not rely on it. Not covered by the methods base (it gives no rule on how directly a question must serve a goal).
- **Q2, mild presupposition.** "Tell me about a time you needed a tool but could not get one." assumes there was such a time. I kept it as fine because the committee needs to hear those stories and the first question (Q1) gives people a way in; Mara should accept "I can't think of one" without pressing.

## Goals and questions
- Goal 1, how neighbours get hold of a tool they need once or twice: Q1 and Q2 with their follow-ups. Covered.
- Goal 2, what people who joined and then stopped borrowing ran into: Q5 and Q6 (Group B), with Q7 and Q8 for everyone. Covered, once the two Q6 follow-ups above are made neutral.
- Goal 3, what has stopped neighbours who have heard of the shelf from coming in: Q3 and Q4 (Group A), with Q7 and Q8, and Q9 as an opinion at the end. Covered.
- Order: warm-up, then facts (Q1 to Q8), then the one opinion question (Q9), then the closing. This follows M2 ("start with the factual questions") and M1. Fine.
- Timing: per person 3 consent + 3 warm-up + 8 + 6 (or 6) + 10 + 4 = 34 minutes, 6 inside the 40-minute limit (M1: keep interviews within an hour; M2: finish with a closing question). Seven core questions and about 13 follow-ups in 24 minutes is a fair load for a first-time interviewer; the 2 spare minutes went to the busiest block. Whether 3 minutes is enough to read the consent script is not checked (not covered by the methods base).

## Checked and fine
- W1 and its follow-ups ("How long have you been here?", "What do you like about the area?").
- Q1 and its follow-ups ("What was the job?", "What did you do then?", "How did that turn out?"). Q2 and its follow-ups ("What happened?", "What did you do instead?").
- Q3 ("What have you heard about the tool shelf at the hall?") and its follow-ups ("Where did you hear about it?", "What did you take it to be?"). Q4 and its follow-ups ("What happened next?", "What did you do in the end?").
- Q5 ("Walk me through how you came to join.") and its follow-ups ("What were you hoping to borrow?", "What happened the first time you came in?"). Q6 itself and its first follow-up ("What happened after that?").
- Q7 and all three follow-ups ("Where do jobs around the house tend to happen?", "Tell me about the last time one of those jobs came up.", "Tell me more about that."). Q8 and its follow-up ("Can you give me an example?"). Q9 ("What would you tell a neighbour who asked you about the tool shelf?") and its follow-ups ("What makes you say that?", "Tell me more about that."). Q9 asks for a view, but it is last and ties to what they did.
- C1 "Is there anything we have not talked about that you think I should know?" is yes/no in form, but it is the usual closing catch-all and M2 supports a closing question. Fine.
- "Is it all right if I record?" is yes/no on purpose; consent needs a clear yes (M2).
- Words match the audience file ("borrow", "tool shelf", "the hall"); none of "inventory", "onboarding", "membership model", "stakeholders" or "lapsed" appears in a question or in the script read aloud. The card is plain and consistent with the follow-ups.
- Sensitive topics: nothing asks about money, income or housing, and the card says to follow the person's lead on health or mobility.
- Recruitment keeps out volunteers and recent borrowers, offers phone or home visit for the step, and gives the voucher whether or not every question is answered.
- The writer's notes in part 8 are honest about what changed and what is still a proposal.
