# Discussion guide: Larkspur Tool Library

Total time: 34 minutes (no one is asked every question; see part 8)
For: Mara Quill, a volunteer who has never run an interview

## 1. What we want to learn
1. How neighbours get hold of a tool they need once or twice (borrow, buy, hire, ask a friend, do without).
2. What people who joined and then stopped borrowing ran into.
3. What has stopped neighbours who have heard of the tool shelf from coming in.

## 2. Who to talk to
Six to eight people, 40 minutes at most each, in the hall's small meeting room, at their home, or by phone. Within six weeks.
- Group A: adults living within about a mile of the hall who have never joined (most will have seen the leaflet or noticeboard).
- Group B: people who joined and have not borrowed for a year.
- Aim for a few from each group. Mixed ages, renters and retired neighbours are all welcome.
- Do not ask: current volunteers and committee members, or anyone who borrowed in the last three months.
- Invite: a short note through letterboxes on the three nearest streets, a card on the hall noticeboard, and a personal message from Mara to lapsed members whose contact details the library holds.
- Thank-you: a £10 voucher for the hardware shop on Alder Road, given whether or not the person answers every question.
- The hall has a step at the front door. Offer the phone or a home visit to anyone who finds steps hard.

## 3. Consent and recording script (read aloud) — 3 min
> "Thank you for making time. My name is Mara and I help at the Larkspur Tool Library. We are talking to neighbours to learn how people get tools for jobs at home, so we can decide how to improve things. Taking part is your choice. You can skip any question, and you can stop at any time, and you will still get the voucher. There are no right or wrong answers; we are only interested in what you really do and think. I would like to record our chat so I can listen again and not miss anything. [Read the next line only once the committee has confirmed it: Only I and the small group of committee members working on this will hear it.] Your name will not be used in anything we write or share. [Read the next line only once the committee has confirmed it: After we have listened, we will delete the recording and keep only typed notes with no names on them.] Is it all right if I record? ... [wait for a yes, then start recording and say: 'It is recording now.'] If they say no: carry on with written notes only, and say so."
This script is for ordinary small-organisation conversations; for a university, school or health service, ask their ethics contact.
[ask them: who may hear the recording, whether the recording is deleted, and how long the typed notes are kept. Do not read the two bracketed lines aloud until the committee has said yes; without them, tell the person only what has been agreed.]

## 4. Warm-up — 3 min
W1. Tell me about living around here.
- Probe: How long have you been here?
- Probe: What do you like about the area?

## 5. Core questions — 24 min total
Q1 and Q2 are for everyone. Q3 and Q4 are for Group A only. Q5 and Q6 are for Group B only. Q7 to Q9 are for everyone. If a question is for the other group, skip it.

### 5.1 Getting a tool they need — 8 min
Serves goal: 1
Q1. Tell me about the last time you needed a tool for a job around the house that you do not use often.
- Probe: What was the job?
- Probe: What did you do then?
- Probe: How did that turn out?

Q2. Tell me about a time you needed a tool but could not get one.
- Probe: What happened?
- Probe: What did you do instead?

### 5.2 Never joined (Group A only) — 6 min
Serves goal: 3
Q3. What have you heard about the tool shelf at the hall?
- Probe: Where did you hear about it?
- Probe: What did you take it to be?

Q4. Tell me about any time the tool shelf crossed your mind when you needed a tool.
- Probe: What happened next?
- Probe: What did you do in the end?

### 5.3 Joined, then stopped borrowing (Group B only) — 6 min
Serves goal: 2
Q5. Walk me through how you came to join.
- Probe: What were you hoping to borrow?
- Probe: What happened the first time you came in?

Q6. Tell me about the last time you borrowed something from the shelf.
- Probe: What happened after that?
- Probe: What made you stop coming in?
- Probe: Tell me about a time you thought of borrowing, then held back.

### 5.4 Fitting it into life — 10 min
Serves goal: 2, 3
Q7. Walk me through a typical week for you.
- Probe: Where do jobs around the house tend to happen?
- Probe: Tell me about the last time one of those jobs came up.
- Probe: Tell me more about that.

Q8. Tell me about what getting to the hall involves for you, from leaving home to getting back.
- Probe: Can you give me an example?

Q9. What would you tell a neighbour who asked you about the tool shelf?
- Probe: What makes you say that?
- Probe: Tell me more about that.

## 6. Closing — 4 min
C1. Is there anything we have not talked about that you think I should know?
Thank them. Say the committee decides in January how to use a small grant and what to put on the next leaflet, and that nothing they said will be linked to their name. Give your contact details. Hand over the £10 voucher. Offer to tell them what the group decides, if they would like that.

## 7. How to run this interview (card)
Before
- Check which group the person is in. Mark which questions to skip.
- Charge the phone or recorder and test it. Bring paper and a pen.
- Choose a quiet place. Offer a home visit or phone call if steps are hard.
- Keep the voucher with you.
During
- Read the consent script word for word. Wait for a yes before recording.
- Ask the question as written. Then stop and wait. Silence is fine; count to five in your head.
- Use the follow-up questions (the lines under each question) only if the answer is short.
- Ask for stories: "tell me about the last time".
- Watch the clock. Move on at the minutes shown. Always keep C1.
Things not to do
- Do not help or explain the tool shelf when someone is stuck. Say "Take your time" or "What do you remember?".
- Do not give your own view, and do not defend the library's hours, fee or stock.
- Do not ask about money, income or housing. If someone brings up health or mobility, follow their lead and do not press.
- Do not say "so you mean...". Use "Tell me more about that".
If
- Someone goes quiet: say "There is no hurry" and repeat the question in other words.
- Someone wanders off topic: let them finish, then say "Thank you. Can I go back to...?"
- Someone wants to stop: stop, say thank you, and still give the voucher.
- Someone asks you a question about the library: say "Good question, I can answer after we finish."
Straight after
- Write down the three things that stood out, while fresh.
- Save notes without names. Do not put the person's name on shared files.

## 8. Notes from the writer
Changes from the committee's draft questions (committee-notes.md):
- "Do you find our opening hours inconvenient?" was a yes/no question that hints at the answer. Replaced by Q7 and Q8, which ask about the person's week and what getting to the hall involves. Opening hours are not named, so people raise them themselves if they matter.
- "How much would you love a weekday evening session and a delivery service?" was leading and double-barrelled. Dropped. Ideas for new services are for the committee to weigh after hearing what people actually do (Q1, Q2, Q6).
- "Why don't you join, given that it's only £20 a year...?" was leading and brought in money, which the brief says not to ask about. Replaced by Q3 and Q4, which ask what they heard and what happened.
- "What do you think of our inventory system and the onboarding process...?" used jargon and was double-barrelled. Replaced by Q5 ("Walk me through how you came to join").
- "Would you borrow more if we had more tools?" was closed and leading. Dropped. Q2 and Q6 ask about real times they did not get what they needed.
- The brief says the committee hopes for "just add a weekday session". This guide does not mention sessions, fees or stock, so people can say what matters to them.
