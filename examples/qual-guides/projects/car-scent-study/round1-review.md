# Review: Tumbledown Car Scents discussion guide

Verdict: CHANGES NEEDED

This is a strong guide. Most questions ask about "the last time", the probes are short and neutral, the order runs facts first, and the consent script covers recording, stopping, privacy and no wrong answers. The card for Rowan is practical.
Five things should change before Rowan uses it: one probe and one question do not follow the guide's own "last time" rule, the consent script still has blanks that Rowan cannot read aloud, the timing has no spare minute, and the plan for extra conversations goes past the brief's limit of eight. Goal 2 and Q3 also do not match what they are meant to answer.
I read all 8 core questions, the warm-up and closing question, and every probe myself. The checker found 0 flags and I agree with it on word patterns; the findings below are about meaning.

## Goals and questions
- Goal 1 (how they ended up with the scent): Q1 and Q2, with Q5's "Where did you get it from?". Covered.
- Goal 2 (last time a scent wore off, or last time they stopped using one): Q4, Q5, Q6 cover wearing off. Stopping has no question (see finding on Q4).
- Goal 3 (what they do about smells at other times; buy or put up with): Q7 and Q8. The "buy or put up with" half is only reached through the leading probe in Q8, so it needs a neutral question.
- Decision in the brief (refill subscription, bigger seasonal range, cheaper starter pack, product page): the guide asks nothing about seasonal scents or a first purchase, and the writer says so. That is allowed by the brief's three goals, and no question asks people to predict. I note it only so the owner knows the interviews will speak to refills, not to the other two options.

## Checked and fine
W1 and its probe; Q1 and its three probes; Q2 and its probes; Q3 probes 1 (as a plain question); Q4 probes; Q5 and its three probes (including "What did it cost?", which is past behaviour); Q6 question and probe 1; Q7 probes; Q8 question and probe 1; C1 and its probe; the consent script wording on stopping, no wrong answers, the £10 voucher and asking for a clear yes; the order (facts first, opinion later); the card in section 7 (waiting, neutral probes, "last time" rule, what not to ask); the topics left out (smoking, pets, income); no jargon from jargon.txt in any question.

## Checker flags I dismissed
- `guide-check` raised no flags.
- `lint` raised 0 flags across 32 questions and probes. Checked by hand: no question mixes two topics with "and", none is yes/no, none uses a word from jargon.txt.
- Q4 probe "How long after you got it was that?" has no flag; it asks for a fact about the past.
- C1, "Is there anything about smells in your car that we have not talked about, but you think I should know?", is long but is one idea, and this is a closing question that M2 supports, so I did not flag it.

## What I could not check
- Whether the timing works in practice. The methods base only says keep interviews within an hour (M1) and leave room for follow-ups; it has no figure for a novice interviewer.
- Whether the plain wording suits real customers. M6 says to test the questions on someone like the participant; this was not done.
- How to ask about smell: only M11, which supports the caution, not a method, so Q3 and its probes are not covered by the methods base.
- The order in which to approach group (a) and group (b): not covered by the methods base.
