# Review: Tumbledown Car Scents discussion guide

Verdict: CHANGES NEEDED

This is a much stronger guide than the first one. All eight findings from the first review are fixed: the leading probes are gone, the questions ask about "the last time", goal 2 now has a stopping question (Q7), the consent blanks are filled from the shop notes, the timing leaves five spare minutes, and the cap of eight is clear.
Two small things remain before Rowan uses it. Goal 4 (words for the smell) is the writer's own addition and its only question is the one Rowan is told to skip first. The closing promises to delete anything on request, which the shop notes do not say the shop will do.
I read all 9 core questions, the warm-up, the closing and every probe myself. The checker found 0 flags and I agree with it on word patterns; the findings below are about meaning.

## Goals and questions
- Goal 1 (how they ended up with the scent): Q1 (what happened, where from, what made them pick it), Q2 (who chose, what else before it), Q5 ("Where did you get it from?"). Covered.
- Goal 2 (last time a scent wore off, or the last time they stopped using one): Q4, Q5, Q6 for wearing off; Q7 for stopping. Covered.
- Goal 3 (what they do about smells at other times; buy or put up with): Q8 (with probe "Tell me about a time you left a smell as it was") and Q9. Both halves are now reached by neutral questions. Covered.
- Goal 4 (words for the smell, for the product page): Q3 only, and Q3 is optional (see finding 1).
- Decision in the brief (refill subscription, bigger seasonal range, cheaper starter pack): no question asks people to predict, and none asks for a preference between the three. The interviews will speak mostly to refills; the writer says so. Allowed by the brief's three goals.
- Order: warm-up, then what they got, then what happened when it stopped, then other smells, then the closing. Facts before opinions (M2). Fine.
- Past behaviour (M7, M8, M10): every core question asks for the last time; none asks "would you". The card forbids "would you pay", "would you buy" and "do you like". Fine.
- Number and length of conversations (M9, M12): up to eight, four in each group, 30 minutes at most, three weeks, which matches the brief's six to eight. The card's rule (read notes after the 6th, then 7th and 8th if still new, ask the owner before going past eight) is believable. The 4/4 split and "start with group (a)" are marked by the writer as suggestions.
- Timing: 2 + 2 + 18 + 3 = 25 minutes for people asked to give 30. Believable. Q4 to Q7 get 9 minutes for four questions and ten probes, which is tight for a first-time interviewer; the card's skip rule (Q3 first, then the last probe of each question) covers it.
- Consent script: covers why you are asking, recording with a clear yes, stopping, skipping questions, privacy, how long things are kept, no wrong answers, and the voucher whether or not they finish. Fine apart from the closing sentence in finding 2.
- Probes: all short and neutral ("What happened next?", "Tell me more", "What did you do instead, if anything?"). Fine.

W1 and its probe; Q1 and its three probes; Q2 and its two probes; Q3 probe ("Where would you have smelled something like it before?"); Q4 and its three probes; Q5 and its three probes (including "What did it cost?", which is past behaviour); Q6 and its probes; Q7 and its probes; Q8 and its three probes; Q9 and its probes; C1 and its probe; the consent script wording on recording, stopping, no wrong answers, notes, the £10 voucher and the clear yes; the plan to take handwritten notes if they say no to recording; the card in section 7 (waiting, neutral probes, "last time" rule, what not to ask, what to do if they go quiet); the topics left out (smoking, pets, children's travel sickness, income); no word from jargon.txt in any question or probe. ## Checker flags I dismissed
- `guide-check` raised no flags: every part is present, the minutes add up, every question has a probe.
- `lint` raised 0 flags across 34 questions and probes. Checked by hand: no question mixes two topics with "and", none is yes/no, none uses a word from jargon.txt.
- "Just to check, you are happy to carry on?" in the consent script is a yes/no question, but it is a consent check where a clear yes is what Rowan needs (M2), so I did not flag it.
- C1, "Is there anything about smells in your car that we have not talked about, but you think I should know?", is long but is one idea and is a closing question that M2 supports.
- W1, "Tell me about a normal week of driving for you.", asks about a usual week rather than a last time. It is a warm-up that asks for facts about driving, not the subject of the study, and its answer is not used for a decision, so I left it.

## What I could not check
- Whether the timing works in practice. The methods base only says keep interviews within an hour (M1) and leave room for follow-ups; it has no figure for a novice interviewer.
- Whether the plain wording suits real customers. M6 says to test the questions on someone like the participant; this was not done (the writer suggests a friendly volunteer).
- How to ask about smell: only M11, which supports the caution, not a method, so Q3 and its probes are not covered by the methods base.
- Whether to approach group (a) before group (b), and whether four in each group is enough: not covered by the methods base beyond M9's six-to-eight figure for similar people.
- Whether the shop will actually honour a deletion request or which address Rowan will use: only the owner knows.
