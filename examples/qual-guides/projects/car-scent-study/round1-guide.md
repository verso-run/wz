# Discussion guide: Tumbledown Car Scents

Total time: 30 minutes
For: Rowan Pell, who has never run an interview, talking to one person by phone or video

## 1. What we want to learn
1. How people ended up with the scent that is in their car now: who chose it, where it was bought, what else they tried.
2. What happened the last time a scent wore off, or the last time they stopped using one.
3. What people do about smells in the car at other times (windows, sprays, cleaning), and whether they see it as something to buy or something to put up with.

## 2. Who to talk to
Six to eight people, 30 minutes at most each, within three weeks (brief, section 6). Plan one or two extra in case answers still surprise you after eight (methods base, "Sample size"; research note, S2).

- Adults who drive regularly and bought any car air freshener in the last twelve months, ours or anyone's (audience, section 1).
- Two groups: (a) people who bought from us once and did not buy again; (b) people who bought something else, such as from a petrol station or supermarket, and never tried us. Aim for some of each. Start with group (a), then add group (b) (research note, answer to the owner's question; the split is a suggestion, not a sourced rule). [ask them: how many of each group they want]
- Do not ask: family, friends, anyone who works in car sales, valeting or the fragrance trade, or anyone who bought from us more than twice (audience, section 2).
- Invite: a short, plain email from Rowan to one-time customers whose address you hold, and a post in a local drivers' group for group (b) (audience, section 3). Invite from the whole list, not only the first people to reply.
- Keep a simple list of who was asked, who said yes and who said no. Include people who are not keen.
- Thank-you: a £10 shop voucher, given whether or not the person answers every question (audience, section 6).
- By phone or video, at a time that suits them, nothing to install.

## 3. Consent and recording script (read aloud) — 3 min
> "Thank you for making time. My name is Rowan, and I run Tumbledown Scent Co. with a colleague. We are a small shop that makes car air fresheners. I am asking you about how you choose and use things for the smell of your car, so we can make better decisions about what we do. I am not selling anything today.
>
> Taking part is up to you. You can skip any question, and you can stop at any time, and you will still get the £10 voucher. There are no right or wrong answers. I am interested in what really happened, even if it is boring or you did not like something.
>
> With your OK, I would like to record the call so I can listen again instead of writing everything down. Only the two of us who run the shop would hear it. Your name will not be used in anything we write or share, and we will not link what you say to you. We will keep the recording and notes only until we have written up what we learned, and then delete the recording. [ask them: how long they will keep the notes, and who the second person hearing it is]
>
> Is it all right if I record? ... (wait for a clear yes before pressing record) ... Thank you. The recording is on now. Just to check, you are happy to carry on?"

If they say no to recording, say "That's fine" and take handwritten notes instead.

This script is for ordinary small-organisation conversations; for a university, school or health service, ask their ethics contact.

## 4. Warm-up — 3 min
W1. Tell me about a normal week of driving for you.
- Probe: What do you usually use the car for?

## 5. Core questions — 20 min total

### 5.1 How the scent got into the car — 6 min
Serves goal: 1
Q1. Tell me about the last time you got something to make your car smell nice. What happened?
- Probe: When was that, roughly?
- Probe: Where did you get it?
- Probe: What made you pick that one?

Q2. Who chose the one that is in your car now?
- Probe: Tell me more about how that came about.
- Probe: What else had you used before it?

Q3. What does it remind you of, if anything?
- Probe: Where would you have smelled something like it before?
- Probe: What is it like in the car when it is new?
- Probe: What is it like a week later?

### 5.2 When a scent wore off or stopped — 8 min
Serves goal: 2
Q4. Walk me through the last time one wore off.
- Probe: How did you notice?
- Probe: What did you do next?
- Probe: How long after you got it was that?

Q5. Tell me what happened after that. What did you do about the smell in the car?
- Probe: What did you get, if anything?
- Probe: Where did you get it from?
- Probe: What did it cost?

Q6. Tell me about the last time you thought about getting another one.
- Probe: What happened?
- Probe: What got in the way, if anything?

### 5.3 Smells in the car the rest of the time — 6 min
Serves goal: 3
Q7. What do you do about smells in the car at other times?
- Probe: Tell me about the last time the car smelled of something you did not want.
- Probe: What did you do then?

Q8. Tell me about the last time you did something about how the car smells, apart from a freshener.
- Probe: What was that?
- Probe: What was it like compared with just putting up with it?

## 6. Closing — 4 min
C1. Is there anything about smells in your car that we have not talked about, but you think I should know?
- Probe: Is there something you expected me to ask?

Thank them. Say what happens next: "I'm talking to a handful of people, then we'll pick what to work on. I'll send your £10 voucher by email today [ask them: how and when the voucher will be sent]. If you want anything deleted, email me at [ask them: Rowan's contact email]."

## 7. How to run this interview (card)
**Before**
- Read the guide once. Check you have a yes to the invitation, a quiet room, and a pen and paper.
- Have your list of who was asked and who agreed ready.
- Test the recording on your own phone or laptop.
- Keep the product page and the pack out of sight. You are here to listen.

**During**
- Read the consent script word for word. Wait for a clear yes before you record.
- Ask one question at a time, then stay quiet. Count slowly to five before you say anything.
- Ask about the last time, not "usually" or "would you". Real stories beat guesses.
- When someone gives a thin answer, use a probe: "tell me more", "what happened next?", "can you give me an example?"
- Write down their own words. Do not turn them into your words.
- If they cannot find the word for a smell, that is normal. Ask what it reminds them of. Do not offer a list of words.
- Watch the time: about 6, 8 and 6 minutes for the three topics. If you run short, drop the last probe on each question, not a whole topic.
- If they raise allergies or headaches, listen and follow their lead. Do not press.

**Things not to do**
- Do not explain the product, defend it, or say what you hope to hear. If they got it wrong, let it stand.
- Do not ask about smoking, pets, children's travel sickness or income unless the person raises it themselves.
- Do not ask "would you pay", "would you buy", or "do you like".
- Do not finish their sentences.

**If**
- They go quiet: wait. Then say "take your time" or "what comes to mind?" Silence is fine.
- They say "I don't remember": say "that's fine, what do you remember?" and move on.
- They ask what you want to hear: "There is no right answer. I want to know what happened for you."
- They want to stop: thank them, stop recording, and still send the voucher.
- They start talking about something not on the guide: let them for a minute, then go back to the question.

**Straight after**
- Within an hour, write five lines: what surprised you, what they did (not what they said they might), any wording they used for the smell.
- Mark whether they are group (a) or (b) and add a line to your list.
- Send the voucher.
- After the 6th conversation, read your notes together and ask whether you are still hearing new things. If yes, do another one or two.

## 8. Notes from the writer
**What I changed from the owners' draft questions** (docs/draft-questions.md)
1. "Would you pay £4 a month for a refill subscription so you never run out?" — asks about the future and says what you hope for. It also uses "subscription", which the audience does not say. Replaced by Q4 to Q6, which ask what people actually did last time a scent wore off and what they bought and paid (methods base, "Past behaviour").
2. "Don't you think seasonal scents are a great idea?" — leading and a yes/no question. No direct replacement. Q1, Q2 and Q5 ask what they picked and why; if seasons come up, probe "tell me more". [ask them: whether to test seasonal scents later with real choices, not an opinion question]
3. "How do you feel about our scents and our packaging?" — two topics in one question, and it asks for opinion first. Dropped; Q3 asks what the scent reminds them of. Packaging can be raised only if the person brings it up.
4. "Why did you not buy a second one, since it only costs £6?" — loaded ("since it only costs £6") and may feel like blame. Replaced by Q6 (the last time they thought about getting another).
5. "Would you buy more if we had more scents?" — hypothetical and yes/no. Replaced by Q1 and Q5, which ask what they picked and what else they tried.
6. "Do you leave the windows open as well as using an air freshener?" — yes/no and suggests an answer. Replaced by Q7.

**On "just ask them what they want" and "I will not know what to say if they go quiet":** the card tells Rowan to wait, gives short probes, and the guide asks about the past.

**Other choices**
- Rowan tends to explain the product when someone hesitates, which puts words in their mouth. The card says so in plain terms, and there are no product details in the questions. If someone else can run the calls, that would help; this is a suggestion, not a requirement.
- Smell is hard to describe (methods base, "Asking about smell (words)"). Q3 asks what it reminds them of, with no word list. No source here gives a method for asking about smell, so this is the writer's suggestion.
- The brief says not to ask about smoking, pets, children's travel sickness or income. None of the questions mention them. Q7 asks about unwanted smells in general; if the person names one of these topics, follow their lead.
- Questions ask about "the last time". Only keep to the last few weeks if the person can remember it; the limit is my inference (research note, Q3).
- 30 minutes: 3 consent, 3 warm-up, 20 core, 4 closing. There are eight core questions; this leaves room for probes.
- The £10 voucher is the shop's own, so people who are fond of the shop may over-represent. Note who agreed and who did not.
- Group (b) have not bought from us. Q1 to Q8 work for both groups because they begin with whichever freshener the person last used.

**Marked [ask them: ...]:** how many of each group; how long notes are kept and who else hears the recording; how and when the voucher is sent; Rowan's contact email; whether to test seasonal scents later.

**Lint and check flags:** all flagged "and/or" questions were reworded or split into separate probes. In Q4 I dropped "or you stopped using one"; if the person stopped without it wearing off, add the probe "Tell me about a time you stopped using one." Goal 2 covers stopping.
